The problem

A tender set runs to hundreds of pages and keeps changing: CfD-I had four amendments in four months, and one removed the ceiling tariff. Bid teams rebuild spreadsheets each time, and nobody can say which page a number came from.

What TenderOS does

It reads the tender once with evidence, keeps the amendments applied, sizes the plant and prices the bid under the tender's own rules, then reviews the bid against those rules and recent awards before you submit.

What you get

The application on your infrastructure, the Excel workbook of every bid, the bid forms and letters, a 15-minute compliance file, and patches and new tender packs under an annual maintenance contract.

3 · Amendments applied: what changed, in SECI's words

The battery becomes mandatory and yoursAmendment-02 · RfS 4.3 · p.1

WasIn case ESS is installed by the RPD as part of the Project, such ESS may be owned by the RPD or may be tied-up separately with a third party by the RPD, for supply of power through Power Exchanges.

NowFor a ‘X’ MW Contracted Capacity, RPD shall mandatorily install ESS of ‘X’ MW/ ‘2X’ MWh capacity as part of the Project. Such ESS shall be owned by the RPD for supply of power through Power Exchanges.

Storage is no longer optional or leasable: for every contracted MW you build and own at least 1 MW / 2 MWh of battery.

No ceiling tariffAmendment-03 · RfS 40.3 · p.4

WasThe Bidder including its Parent, Affiliate or Ultimate Parent or any Group Company will have to submit a single bid (single application) quoting a single “First Round Tariff” in Indian Rupee per kWh for all the Projects applied for. The tariff has to be less…

NowThe Bidder including its Parent, Affiliate or Ultimate Parent or any Group Company will have to submit a single bid (single application) quoting a single “First Round Tariff” in Indian Rupee per kWh for all the Projects applied for. If it is quoted with more than two digits after decimal, digits a…

The ₹5.80/kWh ceiling and the summary rejection above it are removed; one first-round tariff, two decimals.

Settlement: full pass-throughAmendment-03 · RfS 9.3.a.iv · p.2

NowSharing of Profit/ Gains and Losses: The profit/gain and loss arising from the sale of electricity by the RPD through the Power Exchanges shall be settled between the RPD and the CfD Stabilization Fund (“CfD Pool”) in respect of each Time-Block, such that the RPD shall be entitled to receive the S…

The slab sharing (70:30, 50:50, 30:70) goes: the Pool takes the whole gain when the market clears above the strike price and pays the whole shortfall when it clears below, so you net the strike price on every kWh settled. Amendment-01's split above ₹10/kWh is replaced with it.

SECI may award less than 500 MWAmendment-04 · RfS 42.2.b · p.3

Waswill not be eligible to be declared as Successful Bidders.

NowNotwithstanding the above, the final capacity to be awarded under the RfS shall be determined by SECI based on an assessment of the financial viability of the discovered Tariff and the corresponding requirement of the CfD Pool, within the available CfD corpus. Accordingly, SECI reserves the right …

The award is no longer 500 MW by right: SECI will award only what the CfD corpus can carry at the discovered strike price. A high price wins less capacity, or none; the Bid review sizes the Pool's pay-out at your price against the corpus.

9 · What wins: will SECI's CfD Pool carry this price?

Under the CfD the bidder sells in the evening market and SECI's Pool pays (or collects) the difference to the strike price. Amendment-04 lets SECI award only what its ₹76 crore corpus can carry. Net Pool pay-out a year for the full 500 MW:

At ₹8.33 the quote sits at the evening market (₹8.30 in the two chosen hours): the Pool is about neutral on your 100 MW (₹0.2 cr a year paid), so the corpus carries the full 500 MW at the price year's prices; on a lower evening view SECI would weigh the gap.

  • BlocksTender condition not met: Green market within 25% (+75% until month 36)
  • BlocksTender condition not met: 2 MWh/MW every day
  • NoteTender condition to watch: Weekly shortfall within 10%
  • BlocksA strike price of ₹19.92/kWh is 3.3× the highest FDRE peak-power award
  • NoteAt ₹19.92 the Pool pays about ₹62 cr a year for your 150 MW; the full 500 MW would need ₹206 cr a year against a ₹76 cr corpus
  • CostOwn solar is not at its best: 29 MWac instead of 14 lowers the screening price by ₹3.970/kWh
  • CostLittle own RE (14 MWac for 150 MW): nearly all charging power is bought
  • NoteWhat carries the ₹19.92: the battery's capex ₹12.11/kWh, then O&M, insurance, fixed costs and the LC ₹2.53

The over-built case is a pattern bid teams fall into: a battery used only between 0.05% and 50% charge, an extra battery for evening sale, dear battery capex and a 16% equity target. The review names each cost and where to fix it (8 of 13 findings shown).

Firm power carries a clear premium. Wind cleared at ₹3.67–3.85 and solar with storage at ₹2.86–3.13, while four hours of assured peak supply cleared at ₹5.99–6.28 and round-the-clock FDRE at ₹5.25–5.26: storage and firmness, not energy, set the price.
Storage costs keep falling. Assured peak supply dropped from ₹6.27–6.28 (FDRE-VII, Feb 2026) to ₹5.99–6.00 (FDRE-IX, Aug 2026) for the same four-hour product.
Bids cluster tightly. In most auctions the awarded tariffs sit within one to seven paise of each other, because bidders match the lowest price to win capacity. Bidding the right price matters more than bidding low.

Questions

What is TenderOS?

TenderOS is a bid workspace for renewable power tenders in India, made by jouleWise. It reads a tender and its amendments with every value tied to its page, sizes the plant against the tender's conditions, prices the bid for the equity return you target, and produces the bid pack: the financial model as a live Excel workbook, the tender's formats and letters, and the sensitivities for bid day.

Which tenders does it handle?

Firm and dispatchable renewable energy (FDRE) and round-the-clock tenders, assured peak supply, Contracts for Difference (SECI CfD-I), standalone battery storage, and solar or wind with storage, from SECI, NHPC, NTPC and state utilities. A new tender type is added as a tender pack.

Does the AI decide the bid?

No. Language models read documents and draft summaries; every value they read carries its document, page and quote, and a person confirms it before it reaches the bid. Sizing, dispatch and the financial model are deterministic code (HiGHS optimisation and a formula model), and the Excel twin shows every formula.

How accurate is the financial model?

The model is exported as a live Excel workbook in which every number is a formula on the blue input cells. The product's own tests recalculate that workbook in LibreOffice and compare it with the application's numbers, so the workbook a lender reads and the application agree.

Where does the SECI auction data come from?

From SECI's own result notices (https://www.seci.co.in/tenders/results), linked per auction. The dataset is free to download as CSV or JSON from this page. Tariffs are as discovered in the e-reverse auction; SECI sells on to the buying utilities at the tariff plus its trading margin.

What did the model find for SECI CfD-I?

With competitive inputs for a 100 MW bid, the optimiser keeps 10 MWac of own solar and buys the rest of the charging power, for a strike price of ₹8.32/kWh at a 13% equity IRR. The two dearest evening hours averaged ₹8.30/kWh in the price year, so a quote near that leaves SECI's CfD Pool about neutral, which matters because Amendment-04 lets SECI cut the award to what its ₹76 crore corpus can carry.

Can it run on our own servers?

Yes. TenderOS installs with Docker on any cloud or on-premises. The enterprise edition separates an intake zone (tender documents and cloud models) from a core zone on your network where bids, costs and the model live with no cloud client.
