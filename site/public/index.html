      11What wins: will SECI's CfD Pool carry this price?

      The bidder sells in the evening market and SECI's Pool pays, or collects, the difference to the strike price. Amendment-04 lets SECI award only what its ₹76 crore corpus can carry.

      The Pool's net pay-out a year for the full 500 MW, ₹ crore

      At ₹8.33 the quote sits at the evening market (₹8.30 in the two chosen hours): the Pool is about neutral on your 100 MW (₹0.2 cr a year paid), so the corpus carries the full 500 MW at the price year's prices; on a lower evening view SECI would weigh the gap.

      The risks the bidder keeps

      • Deviation settlement (DSM): about ₹0.2 cr a year
      • Availability: each 1% below the declaration costs ₹0.61 cr a year of revenue
      • SECI-designated hours: the two hours you would choose pay ₹8.30/kWh, the evening on average ₹6.73
      • Exchange, transaction and scheduling charges: ₹0.14 cr a year at ₹0.02/kWh
      • Weekly shortfall penalty: 1.5 × (₹10.00 − ₹8.32) on energy beyond 10% short
      • A part award, or none: SECI sizes the award to the corpus at the discovered tariff
      • Not yours: the evening price over 15 years

      The engine

      A state-of-the-art optimiser, not a goal-seek

      A spreadsheet tries a handful of plants. TenderOS states the tender as a linear programme and solves it with HiGHS, the leading open-source solver: solar, wind and battery power and energy chosen together, for the lowest tariff that still earns your return.

      • Every hour of the year. 8,760 hours for the years that bind, or all 25: generation, charging, discharge, state of charge, sale and shortfall in each.
      • The tender's own floors. By block, month and year; peak, off-peak and solar hours; ramp limits; the state-of-charge window; penalties at a multiple of the tariff or hard floors.
      • Real plants. Degradation, battery fade and augmentation, separate sites each with its battery, one or two pooling stations, losses to the billing meter.
      • Minutes, not days. One to two minutes for a 25-year case on four cores, and each run starts from the last, so a changed input re-solves faster.
      • Then proved in 15-minute blocks. 35,040 blocks a year, the compliance workbook the tender's scheduling rules ask for.

      Two zones, by design

      A DMZ (the intake zone) holds only the tender documents and their reading. The core zone holds the bid: inputs, evidence, profiles, sizing, the financial model, the forms and the team.

      The core has no route to the internet

      No cloud keys, no cloud client. It reaches only your own mail server and directory; everything else outbound is blocked, DNS included. The optimiser runs on internal-only container networks.

      The core refuses to read tenders

      The tender-reading routes answer 403 in the core zone. Parsing happens in the DMZ; the reviewed reading moves inward, one way.

      Or read with no cloud at all

      A rule-based reader extracts the headline fields with page and quote without any language model, for sites that allow none.

      Secrets and sign-in

      Passwords hashed with scrypt, sessions in httpOnly cookies, sign-in throttled after ten failures. Provider keys encrypted with AES-256-GCM and shown masked.

      Who sees what

      Administrator and user roles, access granted tab by tab, an activity log kept for 400 days.

      Backups and updates

      Database and files backed up, the newest 14 kept. Updates arrive as bundles signed with an EC P-256 key; the patch tool verifies, backs up, applies and health-checks.

      Modest hardware

      One 4-vCPU, 16 GB server on Ubuntu or Debian with Docker: AWS, Azure, GCP or your own rack. HTTPS only, firewall rules per zone.

      MCP connectors, on subscription

      Connect Claude or another MCP client to TenderOS: 18 tools to look up a condition with its page, set an input, run the optimiser and read the financials. In the two-zone deployment the connectors live in the intake zone, where they reach the tender's reading only, and are switched off in the core.

      Questions

      What is TenderOS?

      TenderOS is a bid workspace for renewable power tenders in India, made by jouleWise. It reads a tender and its amendments with every value tied to its page, sizes the plant against the tender's conditions, prices the bid for the equity return you target, reviews the bid against the tender and recent awards, and produces the bid pack: the financial model as a live Excel workbook, the tender's formats and letters, and the 15-minute compliance files.

      How can months of work become hours?

      Reading and reconciling a tender set, sizing a plant against block-wise floors, building a lender-grade model and proving compliance in 15-minute blocks each take a bid team days or weeks in spreadsheets, and an amendment restarts them. TenderOS reads a tender in minutes, optimises a 25-year case in one to two minutes and regenerates the model, the dispatch and the forms from the same bid, so the team's time goes to judgement: checking the reading, choosing inputs, deciding the price.

      Which tenders does it handle?

      Firm and dispatchable renewable energy (FDRE) and round-the-clock tenders, assured peak supply, Contracts for Difference (SECI CfD-I), standalone battery storage, and solar or wind with storage, from SECI, NHPC, NTPC and state utilities. A new tender arrives as a tender pack.

      Does the AI decide the bid?

      No. Language models read documents and draft summaries; every value they read carries its document, page and quote, and a person confirms it before it reaches the bid. Sizing is linear optimisation (HiGHS), the financial model is formulas, and the bid review is rules. The Excel twin shows every formula.

      Does our bid data leave our premises?

      No. In the enterprise deployment the bid, the costs, the profiles and the model live in a core zone on your network that has no route to the internet. Only the tender documents, which are public, are read in a separate DMZ, with cloud models or with the rule-based reader that uses none.

      What makes the optimiser state of the art?

      It is a linear programme solved with HiGHS, the leading open-source solver, over every hour of the year for the years that bind (or all 25). It chooses solar, wind and battery power and energy together, honours every floor the tender sets by block, month and year, and minimises the tariff for your target equity IRR. A 25-year case takes one to two minutes on four cores, and each run starts from the last.

      Are MCP connectors available?

      Yes, on subscription. An MCP connector lets Claude or another MCP client work with TenderOS through 18 tools: look up a tender condition with its page, list what an amendment changed, set an input, run the optimiser, read the results and the financials. In the two-zone deployment the connectors run in the intake zone and are switched off in the core zone, so the bid's costs and model stay out of reach of any cloud client.

      Can several people work on one bid?

      Yes. Inputs are split into steps by team (Projects, Design, Finance). Anyone can ping a colleague from any input, attach evidence, assign a question and fill in the answer; a super admin signs off each input and the sign-off turns amber if the value later changes. Saves are per user, with versions; an activity log records who did what.

      Are scenarios saved?

      Yes. Every save is a named scenario; each further save is a new version, earlier versions are kept and can be restored, and up to 12 scenarios can be compared side by side.

      How accurate is the financial model?

      The model is exported as a live Excel workbook in which every number is a formula on the blue input cells. The product's own tests recalculate that workbook in LibreOffice and compare it with the application's numbers.

      Where does the SECI auction data come from?

      From SECI's own result notices (https://www.seci.co.in/tenders/results), linked per auction. The dataset is free to download as CSV or JSON from this page. Tariffs are as discovered in the e-reverse auction; SECI sells on to the buying utilities at the tariff plus its trading margin.

      Is the price in the tour the price to bid?

      No. The tour is a worked example on illustrative inputs for a 100 MW bid on SECI CfD-I. Every bid is tender-specific: the price follows your own costs, sites, financing and return. What the example shows is the method: a little own solar beats buying all the charging power, wind raises the price on this tender, and because Amendment-04 lets SECI cut the award to what its ₹76 crore corpus can carry, the bid is tested against the Pool as well as the model.

      Does the tour have a voice-over?

      Yes. Press Play with voice and each screen is narrated while the cursor drives it; the same words appear as captions, and the Voice button turns the sound off. The narration is an AI-generated voice.

      How does it keep up with new tenders and amendments?

      Every day the tender lists of SECI and NHPC and each open tender's page are read, without opening a document, and compared with the day before: a new tender, an amendment or corrigendum, a moved deadline or a result notice is reported, and the drawing at the top of this page is redrawn from the tenders open that day.
